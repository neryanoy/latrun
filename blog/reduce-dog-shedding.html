How to Reduce Dog Shedding: A Simple Weekly Routine

Grooming ·6 min read

First, the honest truth: you can't stop a healthy dog from shedding. Shedding is how dogs replace old and damaged hair. What you can control is where that hair ends up: in a brush you empty into the trash, or on your couch, clothes and floors.

Why dogs shed (and why it's worse in spring and fall)

Many breeds, such as Huskies, Golden Retrievers, German Shepherds and Labradors, have a double coat: a dense, soft undercoat beneath longer guard hairs. Twice a year, these dogs "blow their coat", shedding the undercoat in large amounts as seasons change. Single-coated breeds shed less, but still shed.

Match the tool to the coat

  • Short, smooth coats (Beagles, Boxers): grooming gloves or a rubber brush. These lift loose hair and give a nice massage.
  • Double coats (Huskies, Shepherds, Goldens): a deshedding or slicker brush that reaches into the undercoat.
  • Long, silky coats: a slicker brush plus a comb to prevent tangles.

The simple weekly routine

  1. Brush 2–3 times a week for 5–10 minutes. During shedding season, go daily.
  2. Brush outside when you can, so the fur blows away instead of settling indoors.
  3. Brush in the direction the hair grows, working section by section: back, sides, chest, legs and tail.
  4. Finish with grooming gloves to pick up the loose hair the brush missed.
  5. Roll the furniture once a week with a reusable pet hair roller.

Make brushing something your dog loves

  • Start with short sessions (2 minutes) and end before your dog gets fidgety.
  • Pair brushing with something great: a lick mat with a bit of spread is a perfect distraction.
  • Be gentle around sensitive areas: belly, legs and tail.

Other things that help

  • Nutrition: a complete, balanced diet supports a healthy coat. Ask your vet before adding supplements.
  • Bathing: a bath during shedding season loosens the undercoat. Brush thoroughly once your dog is fully dry. Don't over-bathe, as it can dry out the skin.
  • Hydration: fresh water always available.

Should I shave my double-coated dog?

Generally, no. A double coat helps insulate against both cold and heat, and shaving can affect how it grows back. Regular brushing is the better approach. Ask a professional groomer if you're unsure.

When to see a vet:Shedding that comes with bald patches, redness, sores, scratching or a dull, brittle coat isn't normal shedding. Have your veterinarian take a look.
