Why Does My Dog Eat So Fast? (And 5 Easy Ways to Slow Them Down)

🥣 Feeding · 5 min read

You put the bowl down, turn around, and it's empty. Some dogs treat every meal like a race. It can look funny, but eating very fast often comes with gulped air, coughing, or food coming right back up a few minutes later.

Why some dogs eat so fast

  • Competition: in multi-pet homes, dogs may rush so nobody else gets their food.
  • History: dogs from shelters or large litters may have learned that food doesn't last.
  • Breed and personality: some breeds, like Labradors, are famously food-motivated.
  • Boredom: a bowl is over in seconds. There's no "work" involved.
⚠️ If your dog suddenly starts eating much faster or seems constantly hungry, or if gulping comes with frequent vomiting, a swollen belly or weight changes, see your veterinarian. A bloated, hard belly with restlessness and unproductive retching is an emergency in large, deep-chested breeds.

5 easy ways to slow your dog down

1. Use a slow-feeder insert

A silicone maze that sits inside the bowl you already have. Your dog has to work the food out from between the ridges, which naturally slows each bite. It's the simplest fix because nothing about the routine changes.

2. Scatter the meal into a snuffle mat

Hiding kibble in fleece strips turns dinner into a sniffing game. It slows eating down a lot and provides great mental exercise at the same time.

3. Serve wet food on a lick mat

For wet or soft food, spreading it thinly on a lick mat means it can only be licked up, not gulped.

🥣 Try all three: The Calm & Enrich Kit includes a slow-feeder insert, a snuffle mat and a lick mat, so you can rotate them and keep mealtime interesting.

4. Split meals into smaller portions

Two or three smaller meals a day, instead of one big one, can reduce the urge to gulp. Talk to your vet about the right daily amount for your dog.

5. Feed pets separately

If competition is the issue, feed each pet in a separate room or crate so everyone can eat in peace.

How to introduce a slow feeder

Some dogs get frustrated at first. Start by putting a few pieces of food on top of the ridges so it's easy, and gradually make it harder. Stay nearby the first few times and praise calm eating.

The bottom line

Slowing down mealtime is an easy change that turns a 20-second gulp into a few minutes of satisfying work. Most dogs adapt within a few days.
