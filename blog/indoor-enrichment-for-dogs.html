10 Easy Indoor Enrichment Ideas for Bored Dogs

Behavior & Enrichment · 7 min read

A bored dog finds its own entertainment, usually in your shoes, your couch cushions or the trash. Enrichment means giving your dog's brain a job. The surprising part is that mental work tires dogs out quickly, so 10–15 minutes of sniffing, licking and problem-solving can take the edge off a rainy day with no walk.

1. Snuffle mat dinner

Instead of serving dinner in a bowl, scatter it into a snuffle mat. Your dog has to use its nose to find every piece. Sniffing is one of the most natural and satisfying things a dog can do.

2. Frozen lick mat

Spread plain yogurt, wet food or mashed pumpkin into a lick mat and freeze it for a few hours. A frozen mat can keep a dog busy for 20+ minutes. It's perfect when you're on a work call.

Food safety: Use dog-safe foods only. Avoid anything with xylitol (some peanut butters), chocolate, grapes, raisins, onion or garlic.

3. Slow-feeder meals

For speedy eaters, a slow-feeder bowl turns a 20-second gulp into a few minutes of puzzle-solving at every meal. That's enrichment twice a day with zero extra effort from you.

4. The towel burrito

Lay out a towel, sprinkle treats on it, roll it up and let your dog unroll it. Fold the ends in for a harder version.

5. The muffin-tin game

Put treats in a few cups of a muffin tin and cover every cup with a tennis ball. Your dog has to figure out which balls to remove.

6. Hide and seek

Ask your dog to "stay", hide in another room and call them. Start easy and get trickier. It's great exercise for recall too.

7. Find-it treat trails

Show your dog a treat, say "find it" and toss it. Then hide treats around one room while your dog waits. Increase the difficulty as they get better.

8. Teach a new trick

Five minutes of training is a serious brain workout. Try "touch" (nose to your hand), "spin" or "paw". Keep a treat pouch on you so you can reward at exactly the right moment.

9. The cardboard box shred

Put treats in a cardboard box stuffed with paper and let your dog rip it apart (supervised, and don't let them swallow the cardboard). Many dogs love this one.

10. Calm chew time

Chewing is naturally calming. Offer a size-appropriate chew while you relax together. It's a great way to wind down before bed.

How much enrichment does a dog need?

It depends on age, breed and energy level, but a good starting point is 2–3 short enrichment sessions a day, in addition to walks. Working breeds such as Border Collies and shepherds usually need more. Watch your dog: a relaxed, sleepy dog after a session means it worked.

When to get help:Sudden behavior changes, such as destructiveness, excessive licking or restlessness, can have medical or anxiety-related causes. If you're worried, talk to your veterinarian or a certified trainer.
